## What Ivy is for

<Lede>A home for research communities, built around the questions people work on.</Lede>

Ivy is a network for researchers, organized by research topic. You start from the questions you work on, not a title: claim the topics, see who else is working on the same ones, and find the conferences where that work meets in person.

Behind the network, Ivy is a technology platform to manage both conferences and scientific societies, designed to facilitate community engagement, event discovery, and networking.

## What's next: user research

<Lede>Before building more, talk to researchers. The Mom Test sets the rules for those conversations.</Lede>

The risk with Ivy is building something people say they like but never use. So the next step is interviews with researchers and conference organizers, run the way The Mom Test describes: ask about their lives, not my idea.

- Talk about what they do today, not what Ivy could do for them
- Ask about specific past events, like the last conference they attended or the last collaborator they found, instead of opinions or hypotheticals
- Listen more than I talk, and don't pitch
- Treat compliments as noise; commitments of time, intros, or money are the real signal

The questions start simple. How did you find the last conference you went to? How do you keep up with who else is working on your topic? What did you try that didn't work?

The answers decide what gets built first, and whether a network organized by research topic is the right bet.
