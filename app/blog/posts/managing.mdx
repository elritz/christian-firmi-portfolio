A small authorization microservice that runs standalone or inside a federated GraphQL supergraph. It manages devices, the accounts on each device, and service-to-service calls. The encryption key never leaves the server.

## What it does

<Lede>Dual access and refresh tokens, per device, so one phone can hold several accounts and any of them can be signed out remotely.</Lede>

- Short-lived access tokens on every request, refresh tokens to renew them without interrupting the session
- Device-level identity: track active sessions across devices, revoke one, or sign out everywhere when credentials are compromised
- Profile switching on a single device, with granular sign-out per device or per profile
- Activity monitoring to flag logins from unfamiliar places

## What I learned about microservices

<Lede>They move complexity around more often than they remove it.</Lede>

Isolating functionality made the first version quick to build and let work happen independently. Operating it was the expensive part: inter-service calls, distributed consistency, versioning, service discovery, and debugging across a network. Kubernetes and Docker help with orchestration, and they bring their own upkeep.

The pattern earns its cost when it lines up with a real business domain and a team that can carry the operational load. Without both, a simpler architecture usually wins.
