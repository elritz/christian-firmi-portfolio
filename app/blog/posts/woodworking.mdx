A live-edge coffee table with a black epoxy pour, built at home during the COVID-19 lockdown with the tools I had and a growing curiosity about wood and epoxy. It went wrong once in the middle and came out better for it.

![The finished coffee table](/woodworking/finish_7.png)

## Why

<Lede>Downtime turned into a way to test what I could make in a small space with few resources.</Lede>

The draw was watching raw material become something functional. Every stage taught a technique, and the mistakes taught more than the steps that went to plan.

## The process

<Lede>Prep, pour, disaster, recovery, legs, and six finishes before the right one.</Lede>

### The bad pour, and the fix

The black epoxy did not set the way it should have, and for a while the piece looked finished in the wrong sense. Cutting back, reshaping, and cleaning brought the form back.

### Legs and finish

Four legs gave it stability, then the top went through piano black, a fire finish, black stain, sanded fire stain, and a natural Rubio Monocoat that pulled red out of the grain, before landing on the ghosted finish.
